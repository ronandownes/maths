Length of the sequence

How many fractions are in F₁, F₂, F₃, … ? Is there a pattern in how quickly the lists grow?

The number added at step n is the number of integers from 1 to n − 1 that share no common factor with n except 1.

Why does each new list get longer?

For n ≥ 2, the fraction 1/n is in simplest form and did not occur earlier. So every step adds at least one fraction.

Why are the lengths odd, apart from F₁?

For every fraction a/b below 1/2 there is a matching fraction (b − a)/b above 1/2. The fraction 1/2 sits in the middle. Each pair contributes two terms, so |Fₙ| is odd when n ≥ 2. F₁ has two terms.

The fraction between two neighbours

Take two neighbouring fractions in Fₙ. Add their numerators. Add their denominators. The result is called the mediant.

Extension: prove the neighbour rule

If a/b and c/d are consecutive in a Farey sequence, with a/b < c/d, then bc − ad = 1. The mediant (a+c)/(b+d) lies strictly between them and first appears in Fb+d. Look for these properties in your own examples before reading a proof.
