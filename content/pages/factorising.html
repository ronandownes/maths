Factorising is expanding in reverse. Before trying grouping or quadratics, ask: What is the highest common factor of every term? Use the same systematic method as numerical HCF.

1. Start with numbers — divide out common factors

To find the HCF of 12 and 18, divide both numbers by a common factor, and repeat until the two remaining numbers have no common factor greater than 1.

Start1218
÷ 269
÷ 323
HCF = 2 × 3 = 6

We divide the numbers together at each stage—not one number at a time. This method is particularly helpful when the HCF is not immediately obvious.

2. Two algebraic terms — divide everything by the HCF

Worked example: two terms

12x³y² + 18x²y³

The numerical HCF is 6. For each letter, use the lowest power present in every term: x²y².

HCF = 6x²y²
12x³y² ÷ 6x²y² = 2x
18x²y³ ÷ 6x²y² = 3y
12x³y² + 18x²y³ = 6x²y²(2x + 3y)

Check: multiply the factor back into each term. Do we recover the original expression?

3. Three terms — exactly the same procedure

Worked example: three terms

18x³y² + 24x²y³ − 30x²y²
HCF of 18, 24, 30 = 6
Common letters = x²y²
18x³y² ÷ 6x²y² = 3x
24x²y³ ÷ 6x²y² = 4y
−30x²y² ÷ 6x²y² = −5
6x²y²(3x + 4y − 5)

For any number of terms, divide every term by the same HCF. If a letter is absent from any term, it is not part of the common factor.

4. Grouping — look for a common bracket

Your classroom routine: Have the first two terms something in common? If yes, group the first two and last two. Open two brackets. Take out the HCF of each pair. Check that the brackets match; then swing back and take the common bracket out.

Worked example: grouping

6x² + 9xy + 4xz + 6yz
  1. Group the first pair and the second pair: (6x² + 9xy) + (4xz + 6yz).
  2. Take out each pair's HCF: 3x(2x + 3y) + 2z(2x + 3y).
  3. Both brackets are (2x + 3y).
  4. Factor out the common bracket: (2x + 3y)(3x + 2z).
  5. Expand the answer to check all four terms.

5. When the brackets are reversed: take out a negative

b − a = −(a − b)

Sometimes the second bracket contains the same terms in the opposite order. Pull out a negative to reverse both signs and make the brackets identical.

Worked example: negative HCF

xy − xz + 3z − 3y
  1. Group: (xy − xz) + (3z − 3y).
  2. Take out each HCF: x(y − z) + 3(z − y).
  3. Notice z − y = −(y − z); the second pair needs a negative.
  4. Rewrite as x(y − z) − 3(y − z).
  5. Factor the common binomial: (y − z)(x − 3).
