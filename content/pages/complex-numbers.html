A complex number has two coordinates but is one number. Begin with the picture, name the parts, and then make sense of the calculations.

1. From the number line to the Argand plane

On an ordinary number line, the number 3 appears at a point. On an Argand diagram, the complex number z = 2 + 3i appears at the point (2,3). The horizontal coordinate is the real part; the vertical coordinate is the coefficient of the imaginary unit i, where i² = −1.

Reading a graph and writing a number in rectangular form are two representations of the same idea. A number a + bi corresponds to the point (a,b).

2. Add, subtract and multiply by a real number

Compare corresponding coordinates

Let z = 2 + 3i and w = 1 − 2i. Then z+w = 3+i and z−w = 1+5i. If k=2, then kz=4+6i. Each calculation can be checked on the Argand plane.

3. Multiply in rectangular form

The same distributive law used in expanding brackets applies to complex numbers. The important extra building block is i²=−1.

(2+3i)(1−2i)=2−4i+3i−6i²=8−i

This connects directly to the place-value multiplication app: there are still four partial products. The resulting real and imaginary terms must then be collected separately.

4. Divide in rectangular form

To remove i from the denominator, multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator.

(2+3i)/(1−2i) = ((2+3i)(1+2i))/((1−2i)(1+2i)) = (−4+7i)/5

The divisor must be non-zero. Notice that the denominator becomes a real number.

5. Reason and communicate

  1. Plot −3+2i and write the corresponding ordered pair.
  2. Calculate (2+3i)+(1−2i) and check the new coordinates.
  3. Explain to a partner why i²=−1 changes the real part of a product.
  4. Find the conjugate of 3−4i. Why is the product of a non-zero complex number and its conjugate real and positive?
Reveal answers and explanations
  1. (−3,2).
  2. 3+i, corresponding to (3,1).
  3. The product of the imaginary terms contains i², which equals −1, so that product is real.
  4. 3+4i; multiplying gives 3²+4²=25, and more generally (a+bi)(a−bi)=a²+b²>0 when the number is not zero.
