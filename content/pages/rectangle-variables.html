Change the width and height. Watch the perimeter and area. Each grid square is 1 cm².

1. Move the sides

Choose Rectangle. Drag a red handle or use the width and height sliders. Both results update immediately. The prediction question is optional.

Open Rectangle Explorer in a separate tab

On a small screen, open the app in a separate tab for the largest diagram and controls. One grid square represents one square centimetre.

2. Read the expressions from the shapes

Square: one changing measurement

All four sides have length s. The perimeter is s+s+s+s=4s. If s=5 cm, the perimeter is 20 cm. The area is s²=25 cm².

Rectangle: two changing measurements

Let the width be w and the height be h. Opposite sides are equal, so P=w+h+w+h=2w+2h=2(w+h). For w=8 cm and h=5 cm, P=26 cm and A=wh=40 cm².

The quantity w+h is the half-perimeter. Symmetry makes the shortcut P=2(w+h) possible: one width and one height make a pair, and the pair occurs twice.

3. Increasing and diminishing a product

Keep h fixed and increase w. The perimeter increases by 2 cm for every extra centimetre of width, and the area increases by h cm². Reducing a positive side length diminishes the area. The app lets you see both changes while the other measurement stays fixed.

4. A practical extension: paint on room walls

A rectangular room has length L, width W and height H, all measured in metres. Before subtracting doors and windows, the total area of its four walls is H(L+W+L+W)=2H(L+W) square metres. The bracket L+W is the half-perimeter of the floor plan.

When both dimensions are expressions

Suppose one wall has a total length of (x+5) metres and a height of (y+2) metres. Its area is

(x + 5)(y + 2) = xy + 2x + 5y + 10

Here x and y represent measured lengths in metres. Each bracket describes one complete side of the wall. This is why multiplying two brackets can arise in a practical area calculation. Subtract the areas of doors and windows separately.

5. A measurement with a minimum

Suppose the depth of a trapezoidal prism must be at least 5 cm. If x centimetres represents the extra depth beyond that minimum, then d=x+5 cm, where x≥0. The expression includes a variable amount and a fixed amount. The prism's trapezoidal end requires its own measurements; that is a later extension.

6. Predict, reflect and transfer

  • Before: predict a perimeter if you want a challenge.
  • During: change one side and record what happens to P and A.
  • After: close the app and answer independently: a rectangle is 9 cm wide and 4 cm high. Find its perimeter, half-perimeter and area.
Reveal the exit-check answer

P=2(9+4)=26 cm, half-perimeter 9+4=13 cm, and A=9×4=36 cm².
