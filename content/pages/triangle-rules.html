Identify the given information before choosing a rule. This lesson combines geometric reasoning, clear rule selection, and a draggable triangle.

1. Recognise the situation

Cosine Rule — find a side

Find the length of the side opposite an included angle. Two sides and the angle between them are known.

a = √(b² + c² − 2bc cos A)

Use the square-root form directly.

Cosine Rule — find an angle

All three sides are known (SSS).

A = cos−1((b² + c² − a²) / (2bc))

Sine Rule

At least one matching side–opposite angle pair is known.

a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C

When two angles are given, first check whether you need to calculate the third using A + B + C = 180°.

3. Questions to check understanding

  1. Two sides are 7 cm and 11 cm, with an included angle of 60°. Which rule finds the opposite side?
  2. All three sides are known. Which rule finds an angle?
  3. You know a side and its opposite angle, plus one other angle. Which rule finds another side?
  4. Why can the Sine Rule sometimes give two possible triangles when finding an angle?
Reveal solutions
  1. Cosine Rule; the opposite side is √(7² + 11² − 2 × 7 × 11 × cos 60°) = √93 ≈ 9.64 cm.
  2. Cosine Rule (three sides).
  3. Sine Rule (opposite pair available).
  4. Because sin θ = sin(180° − θ); the supplementary angle may also satisfy the angle-sum constraint.

Given sides in the app are whole numbers from 1 to 20. Given angles are whole degrees from 1° to 179°. The displayed calculated answer can be a decimal.
