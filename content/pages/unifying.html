The Unifying Strand explains the mathematical habits used throughout the Book and Apps. It is not an additional content strand. Whenever you meet a new method, ask what the mathematics means, how else it can be represented, and where the ideas might lead.

1. Building blocks

What ideas, language and procedures do we need? In place-value multiplication: factors, terms, powers and the distributive law.

2. Representation

How can we express the same idea with symbols, words, tables, physical models, graphs or diagrams?

3. Connections

Link number multiplication, algebraic expansion, partial products, area and vertical columns. Mathematics should not consist of unrelated tricks.

4. Problem solving

Understand the question, choose a model or strategy, work through it, interpret the result and evaluate whether it makes sense.

5. Generalisation and proof

Find patterns, formulate general statements and explain why a claim is true. Use a counterexample to disprove a false general statement.

6. Communication

Explain aloud and in writing, label diagrams, use correct notation, listen, question, justify and work with a partner.

One example: place-value expansion

Consider (x+5)(x+2)=x²+7x+10.

Building blocks: distribute all terms. Representation: compare an area model with columns. Connections: relate to (10+5)(10+2). Problem solving: work backwards from a rectangular area. Generalisation and proof: justify the four partial products. Communication: explain each stage to a partner, then swap roles.

Open Place-Value Multiplication

Communication challenge

Working in pairs, one student shows a diagram and the other uses algebra. Each explains their method without immediately giving the final answer. Compare how the representations help, then write a joint justification. A student who finds speaking difficult may communicate first through diagrams, written steps or annotations.

Beyond today's topic

Multiplication can be organised by powers because each place has a meaning. Later, that same organisation helps with polynomial division, including synthetic division where appropriate. We can point towards that development without teaching it prematurely.
