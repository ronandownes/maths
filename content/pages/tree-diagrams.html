Multiple eventsMultiplication ruleWith or without replacement

A probability tree keeps track of the outcomes of a multi-stage experiment. The probabilities leaving each node must sum to 1.

1. Multiply along a branch

Suppose a bag contains 3 red counters and 2 blue counters. Two counters are drawn without replacement.

Find the probability of two red counters

First red: 3/5. After drawing a red counter, the bag contains 2 red and 2 blue counters. Second red, given first red: 2/4.

P(RR) = (3/5) × (2/4) = 3/10

Multiplication along a branch gives the probability of that complete sequence of events.

2. Add mutually exclusive paths

For exactly one red, two distinct paths are possible: RB or BR.

P(RB or BR) = (3/5)(2/4) + (2/5)(3/4) = 3/5
With replacement: the contents of the bag are restored after the first draw, so the probabilities on the second stage remain 3/5 and 2/5. Without replacement: the composition changes.

3. Independent practice

A bag contains 4 green and 1 yellow counter. Two are drawn without replacement.

  1. Find P(GG).
  2. Find P(GY).
  3. Find the probability of exactly one yellow counter.
Reveal solutions
  1. (4/5)(3/4) = 3/5.
  2. (4/5)(1/4) = 1/5.
  3. P(GY) + P(YG) = 1/5 + (1/5)(4/4) = 2/5.
